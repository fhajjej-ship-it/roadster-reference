# Read a performance claim properly

[Home](../README.md) · [Claims](claims.md) · [Physics lab](physics-lab.md)

## 0–60 mph is not 0–100 km/h

Using exact unit definitions, 60 mph = **96.56064 km/h**. Reaching 100 km/h means continuing to a higher speed. Do not relabel one time as the other.

## Rollout changes what the clock measures

Some acceleration figures exclude the time taken to travel the first foot. That makes the published number smaller than an elapsed time measured from rest. The adjustment depends on the actual early motion; do not add a universal correction.

The [Hagerty interview, 16:28 onward](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=22rvpUecLts&t=988s) explicitly qualifies its 0.55-second figure as using rollout. Its narration distinguishes Performance, Thrust and Rocket figures. The [official reveal, 18:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qt5p-_tE8d0&t=1110s) announces 0.81 seconds. Car and Driver reports 0.8 seconds to one decimal place, while Top Gear’s coverage uses a 0.59-second figure. These are not interchangeable measurements. [Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/a74145531/tesla-roadster-revealed/) · [Top Gear](https://www.topgear.com/car-news/electric/tesla-roadster-has-finally-landed-thrusters-0-60mph-059s-and-flight)

The differences could reflect protocol, configuration, rounding, run or target. The reviewed evidence does not resolve all of those differences. Calling them different reports does not establish that the underlying engineering claims contradict each other.

## What an independent result should disclose

| Dimension | Useful disclosure |
|---|---|
| Vehicle | Variant, hardware, software, production/prototype status, mass |
| Timing | Instrument, start trigger, rollout, finish speed, raw trace |
| Surface | Slope, preparation, grip, weather, wind and temperature |
| Tires | Model, pressure, wear, temperature and road/track status |
| Energy state | Battery charge and temperature; thruster initial/final conditions |
| Repetition | Multiple runs, cooldown/refill needs and spread |
| Independence | Who controlled setup, recorded data and reviewed the result |

This is an editorial standard for evaluating future evidence, not a report that these tests have been conducted.

## Average acceleration is not peak acceleration

```text
average acceleration = change in speed / elapsed time
```

At 60 mph reached in 0.85 s from actual rest, the average would be about **3.22 g**. Peak acceleration could be higher or lower at particular points. A rollout-subtracted time does not describe the entire from-rest interval, so our calculator’s result is only a scenario if that number is used as its time input.

The calculation does not model jerk, tire deformation, battery current, control response or occupant loading. It cannot validate a claimed test or predict injury risk.

## Range figures require a cycle

EPA range involves laboratory testing and adjustments. It is not simply battery capacity divided by an arbitrary efficiency figure. WLTP, EPA, constant-speed tests and a manufacturer’s unspecified target can produce different numbers. [EPA’s official explanation](https://www.epa.gov/greenvehicles/fuel-economy-and-ev-range-testing)

Do not write “EPA range” for the new Roadster unless an applicable rating is actually located. This research did not establish one. Likewise, 300-plus miles in a press report does not establish range with fans or thrusters in continuous use.

## Comparisons are context, not a leaderboard

Rimac lists different numbers for 0–60 mph and 0–100 km/h on its [Nevera R page](https://www.rimac-automobili.com/NeveraR/). McMurtry’s [fan-car demonstration](https://mcmurtry.com/upside-down-driving/) shows a relevant mechanism. Neither source supplies a controlled comparison against the redesigned Roadster. Do not award a fastest-car title by sorting unmatched marketing figures.
