# Compare the Roadster versions

[Beginner’s guide](start-here.md) · [Home](../README.md) · [Claims and sources](claims.md)

Tesla describes three versions of the October 2026 Roadster: **Performance, Thrust and Rocket**. Compare their equipment, seats and intended use below, then see what changed from the 2017 concept.

## The differences at a glance

These are **Tesla’s descriptions**, not independently verified production-car specifications. All three use electric motors to turn the wheels.

- **Performance — two seats, systems that help with grip.** Moving wings and fans help press the car toward the road so the tires can grip it.
- **Thrust — two seats, an extra forward push.** Fans are paired with rear gas thrusters that add a forward push.
- **Rocket — one seat, pushes in several directions.** Fans are paired with gas thrusters around the car for movement through the air, including upward movement and turning.

Source: [Tesla’s product page](https://www.tesla.com/roadster), version descriptions and footnotes reread October 17, 2026. These summaries highlight differences; they are not complete equipment lists. Features and performance figures elsewhere on Tesla’s page do not necessarily apply to all three versions.

## What the equipment terms mean

- **Active aero** means parts, such as wings, that move to change how air pushes on the car. Moving them changes the balance between grip and air resistance.
- **Fan suction** means fans remove air from beneath the car. Higher surrounding air pressure then presses it toward the road, helping the tires grip. The downward push is called **downforce**.
- **Cold-gas thrusters** release pressurized gas without burning fuel. Gas goes one way and pushes the car the other, like a released balloon. Rear thrusters provide a forward push; **perimeter** means around the car’s outer edges, allowing pushes in different directions.

The [engineering chapter](engineering.md) explains these principles, their limits and sources.

## What Tesla says about access and road use

- **Performance — “By reservation.”** Tesla accepts reservations; this wording does not give a confirmed delivery date.
- **Thrust — “Limited release.”** This label does not say how many vehicles will be available or guarantee access.
- **Rocket — “Invite only.”** Tesla also says this version is **not approved for public road use**. Do not treat its airborne demonstration as a feature available for everyday road driving.

Tesla calls Thrust and Rocket **augments**, meaning additions or upgrades. The final ordering options remain unresolved. Footnotes restrict some features to Rocket and describe optional Thrust equipment. The Rocket restriction does not establish the other versions’ road approval. See [availability and reservations](availability.md).

## What changed from the 2017 concept?

The 2017 concept was an earlier proposed design. Do not use its advertised numbers to fill gaps in the redesign’s specifications.

| Topic | 2017 announcement | 2026 reviewed information |
|---|---|---|
| Distance on a charge (range) | Advertised 620 miles | Reports cite an event claim above 300 miles; an official test-based range rating remains unconfirmed. |
| Seats | Advertised four | Two or one, depending on the version. |
| Battery energy | Proposed 200 kWh | The reviewed interview does not disclose the final amount available for driving. |
| Motors turning the wheels | Three motors in launch reporting | Two rear motors described in the engineering interview. |
| Production timing | Originally expected in 2020 | Reported target: 2028; this is not a confirmed customer delivery date. |

**kWh** means kilowatt-hours, a measure of stored energy. **Usable battery capacity** is the portion available to operate the car. Neither number alone tells you driving range, because energy use per mile varies.

Sources: [Original Tesla reveal, 29:39–33:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5RRmepp7i5g&t=1779s), [2017 launch report](https://arstechnica.com/cars/2017/11/not-just-a-semi-announcement-tesla-promises-a-new-roadster/), [engineering interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=22rvpUecLts&t=161s), [Reuters](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/teslas-new-roadster-turns-air-spacex-thrusters-wont-land-until-2028).

## What the demonstration does and does not tell us

At the **October 15, 2026 reveal in McGregor, Texas**, witnesses reported an unmanned vehicle driving upside down and moving through the air. A **prototype** demonstrates or tests ideas; its stunts do not establish what a customer car can do. [Reuters](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/teslas-new-roadster-turns-air-spacex-thrusters-wont-land-until-2028) · [Top Gear’s eyewitness report](https://www.topgear.com/car-news/first-look/wow-new-tesla-roadster-can-actually-fly)

Acceleration times differ across reports. Check the version and timing method before comparing them. The [measurement guide](testing.md) and [interview notes](../research/interview-notes.md) explain why the smallest reported number is not a verified production-car record.
